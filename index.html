About

I study human nature — the mechanisms designed by natural selection, and how they function.

I'm a behavioral scientist in San Diego. I received my PhD from the Department of Psychology at the University of Chicago in 2024, working with Alex Shaw, and recently completed a postdoctoral fellowship at UC San Diego with Christine Harris and Michael McCullough. Before graduate school, I studied philosophy at UC Berkeley, then evolutionary psychology with Leda Cosmides and John Tooby at UC Santa Barbara.

I pursue several lines of research: the functions of social emotions like anger, hatred, envy, shame, guilt, and pride; forgiveness, including third-party forgiveness; and morality, moral judgment, friendship, and alliances.

Recently, envy and status are the lines of research I've been most interested in. On envy, I'm developing a novel theory of the emotion, along with two empirical papers testing that model — and, with Christine Harris and Michael McCullough, the Fear of Being Envied (FOBE) Scale, measuring the other side of the envy coin. On status, I'm building agent-based models demonstrating how minds track social status, with implications for international relations — the fingerprints of human nature show up even there.

With Alex Shaw and Juliana Schroeder, I have investigated the understudied phenomenon of third-party forgiveness, with an eye toward understanding why it is evaluated so differently from victim forgiveness. With Daniel Sznycer and Patrick Durkee, I conducted preregistered critical tests of two prominent, competing theories of shame and guilt with data from two cultures, using structural equation modeling to contrast the theories' predictions.

Lately I've been bringing the same measurement toolkit to problems outside the lab: the evaluation of language models, where benchmark design is a psychometric problem; AI-text detection; and sports analytics. I'm also an Associate Editor at Open Inquiry in Mental Health (Open Therapy Institute).

Projects

in development

WTR-Bench

A psychometric benchmark for welfare-tradeoff consistency in language-model agents: a validated item bank, an adaptive titration sampler, and variance decomposition across prompt perturbations, so that a model's score comes with an honest confidence interval.

measurement

Detecting AI-generated prose

A controlled program measuring which features of prose drive AI-text detector classifications, against a documented baseline of human writing. A validity study of detectors from the measurement side, including false-positive risk for particular human styles.

simulation

Status and coalition dynamics

Agent-based models in which status is treated as a prediction about who will side with whom, with model predictions tested against real-world interstate-conflict data.
