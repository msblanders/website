The neutralization theory of hatred

A functional account of hatred as an emotion distinct from anger

Aaron Sell · Coltan Scrivner · Mitchell Landers · Anthony C. Lopez

Read PDFDownload PDFPublisher page

Published Oxford Handbook chapter.

Overview

This chapter proposes that hatred and anger address different adaptive problems. In the neutralization theory, hatred responds to cues that another person’s continued presence imposes net costs. The theory predicts that hatred can motivate avoidance or actions aimed at reducing the target’s capacity to impose those costs.

The chapter develops predictions about hatred’s triggers, internal representations, and behavioral consequences. It presents a theoretical framework and identifies hypotheses for empirical testing.
