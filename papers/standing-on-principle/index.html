Standing on principle

How moral principles can excuse disloyalty

Mitchell Landers · Peter DeScioli · Alex Mackiel · Alex Shaw

Read the publication Read the abstract at Stony Brook

Overview

Across seven experiments—four in the main article and three in the supplement—participants judged people choosing sides in conflicts involving their friends. The studies varied whether friends were morally right or wrong and whether opposition was motivated by moral principles or other considerations.

People excused opposing a friend when the decision followed the side-taker’s own moral principles. Other motives, or merely appealing to other people’s moral judgments, did not provide the same excuse for disloyalty.
