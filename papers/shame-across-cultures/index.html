Shame across cultures

Cross-cultural regularities and variability in the emotion of shame

Mitchell Landers · Daniel Sznycer

Read PDFDownload PDFPublisher page

Author manuscript; marked forthcoming in the supplied version.

Overview

Shame appears across cultures, yet the situations that elicit it and the ways people express it vary. This chapter examines how an adaptationist account can explain both patterns: a system that manages the threat of social devaluation can produce different responses when local audiences, values, and circumstances differ.

The chapter argues that cultural variation and cross-cultural regularities can be understood within a shared functional account of shame. It is a theoretical synthesis rather than a report of a new experiment.
